# Functional test with GLM 5.3: retail ransomware, customer data leak, payment compromise

Night of 28 to 29 September 2026. CrisisMaker was driven end to end in a real browser (headless
Chromium via Playwright) with a real AI provider: **Ollama Cloud, model `glm-5.3`**, through the
app's own Settings. Everything the players would receive was generated by the app itself.

## The brief

- Client: Maison Aubray Retail Group (fictional French omnichannel retailer: 420 stores, e-commerce,
  6 million loyalty customers). Sector: Retail.
- Crisis: ransomware on head-office IT + theft and threatened publication of the customer database +
  strong suspicion that the payment chain is compromised (skimmer in the online checkout).
- Frame: 3 hours of play, simulated Friday 27 November 2026 (Black Friday week) 08:00 to 11:00,
  Europe/Paris, **4 cells, 15 players**, English.
- Learning objectives per cell and a dated incident timeline were entered in Context
  (see `exercise/operation-frozen-till.json`, fields `learning_objectives` and `attack_path`).

## What was found and fixed (on `claude/bold-hawking-sjakgb`)

### Running on GLM 5.3 / Ollama Cloud
- **Every agent call failed at first.** Non-streamed Ollama calls stay silent for a minute or more,
  and a relay or proxy cuts them (HTTP 502 after 30 s here; Cloudflare and corporate proxies do the
  same). All Ollama JSON calls are now streamed, then assembled, in the app and in the video studio.
- **GLM 5.3 never finished an agent step.** Asked not to reason (`think: false`), it writes its
  reasoning in the answer until the 8,000-token limit. GLM and gpt-oss now reason briefly apart
  from the answer (`think: "low"`: GLM 5.3 Flash went from 123 s and a failure to 5 s), and any
  other model that behaves like this is retried once the same way. Measured on
  deepseek-v4.1-flash, kimi-k2.6, gpt-oss:120b, glm-5.3-flash, glm-5.3 with the real agent prompt.
- Almost-JSON (raw line breaks, quotes in HTML attributes, trailing commas) is repaired, and an
  unreadable reply is asked again once, instead of losing an inject or a challenge.
- The agent: harmless slips in its replies (an argument next to `arguments`, a reason too long, a
  final answer without its lists) are fixed before the strict check; it sees the whole timeline
  (60 injects) instead of paging; older results are shortened before the latest one.
- Settings: the header says "AI unreachable" when the test or the model list fails (it said
  "AI connected" with a dead local server); the key field says it is kept for the browser session
  only; one technical-log entry per error, and 8,000 characters of a failed reply.

### Quality of the exercise the AI builds
- **31 of 34 injects carried the template's demo content** (emails signed by the StonaWave CISO
  with its CEO in copy; the ransom demand kept a pharmaceutical supplier's EDI email as body). An
  inject written by AI now starts blank, with its sender from its actor and its date from the
  simulated clock; every channel is asked for its exact template fields (SMS, memo, external
  email, TV had no field list at all); authority emails follow the sender (CNIL, ANSSI, police),
  not always a CERT-FR vulnerability alert.
- **Times**: planned inject times were read in one convention and written in another, so injects
  were stacked at the end of a phase; the main events of the incident timeline were placed 20 to
  45 minutes early. The agent now works in one convention, gets the incident timeline already in
  exercise minutes, sets main events itself (new tool), and an event whose text says "09:30" is
  placed at 09:30. A check flags any main event away from its time.
- **Channels fit the sender**: 22 of 34 injects were internal emails, including the police, the
  CNIL and the bank. Now: authorities use authority emails, outside organisations external email.
- Dates and times: fields that show the clock follow the simulated time; other dates (a press
  dateline, a tweet) are written in the template's own style.
- Plain text written by AI keeps its characters ("IT &amp; Cyber Cell" showed in email headers).
- The consistency check compares players and cells with the Context frame and asks for the cells
  named in the learning objectives.

### Features that did not work
- **Slide debrief: "Write the debrief with AI" ended without error but left every message empty.**
- **Check & Challenge: the AI challenge was lost on reload** (now saved with the project).
- **Exports: the TV inject was dropped from the ZIP** when the browser cannot read its video, and
  every exported TV image had an empty ticker; a long time value broke the TV layout. Webfonts
  were missing from every exported image (32 font 404s per export).
- Excel import added the file's injects to the open exercise (duplicates): it now goes into a new
  project by default.
- Story debrief: ~70 SVG errors per timeline (globe lines), time labels "J-24" in English.
- Video debrief: silent AI requests cut by proxies, JSON failures, cut titles, "ma-video.mp4".

### Found by two QA agents (Play, Evaluation, Debrief, design tabs) and fixed
- Play: inject numbers changed when an inject was added during the run (log and printed injects no
  longer matched); quick filters went stale while the clock ran; Space started the clock under the
  Settings drawer; a clock jump stacked 5 toasts; "Exercise complete" never showed.
- Data integrity: a deleted cell came back after reload; undo did not restore cells or the written
  inject; dragging an inject to another cell's row did nothing; deleting an actor silently gave its
  injects to someone else; multi-cell injects kept a deleted cell.
- Project safety: New and Load demo wiped the project without asking; loading a library scenario
  mixed two exercises; an invalid file opened a second file picker; the checklist was lost on
  reload; an empty project scored 98/100.
- Translation: the Evaluation tab, the Story debrief editor (always French), the three storyline
  tabs, Check & Challenge and the Slide debrief are now in French and German too.
- Ergonomics: the timelines cut the last minutes off at the right edge; the assistant button hid
  "Modify with AI"; Esc now closes the dialogs, the welcome screen and Settings; the welcome screen
  lists the real tabs and opens them; blank projects no longer start as "Pharmaceutical /
  Ransomware / New York".

### Second pass: code review and a regression sweep of the merged app
After the fixes above, a review agent read the night's diff and a QA agent replayed every tab in
English, French and German at three widths (no AI). What they found, now fixed:
- **Security**: the plain-text clean-up of AI fields had opened a path for HTML in social post
  bodies; post bodies and reaction labels are sanitized again.
- **Undo** restored whole injects, so undoing a move also undid a later text edit of the same
  inject; it now puts back only what the step changed (and keeps images by reference, not copies).
  Adding or deleting a player and adding an actor were not undoable at all, and Ctrl+Z undid an
  earlier change instead.
- **Open**: Chrome refused the file picker (the `.crisismaker.json` extension is too long for it),
  so the app never kept the opened file; opening a file switched the interface to the file's
  language; Open replaced the project without asking.
- AI writing of an inject: the history kept the new date instead of the previous one, and a failed
  call left a changed date (checked again with GLM 5.3: history 08:02, new version 08:32).
- The saved AI challenge was kept after the exercise changed; the Excel import target could change
  during the import; an event text naming another day ("D+1 09:30") was placed on the day played.
- Translation: template field labels in the inject editor, the Outlook ribbon of email previews,
  dates in US format under FR/DE, a few leftover labels; the library says "injects" like the rest.
- Story debrief: the timeline stayed blank with no message when unpkg.com is unreachable (it loads
  React from there); it now says why.
- The demo project contradicted itself: five email images were identical (Thomas Bergmann's
  emails signed by Sophie Delacroix), every email carried the real clock instead of the simulated
  one, the attacker sent the internal crisis-bridge SMS. Each demo inject now has its own sender,
  time and content.
- Narrow screens and long labels: Cells & actors and the library cards overflowed at phone width,
  the Main storyline squeezed six phases into 15 px slivers (it now scrolls inside the timeline),
  French labels were cut in the storyline toolbar and the main events row.

## Tests

- `node --test tests/*.test.js`: 102 pass (80 at the start of the night; the new ones cover the fixes).
- Browser smoke tests (`tests/*-browser-smoke.cjs`): agent, play, scenario builder and video pass;
  several were extended (stable numbers, live filters, exercise end, drag to another cell, undo of
  a deleted cell, undo of an added player).

## Left open

- Variance of the builder agent: on two runs, one renamed the cells after the objectives by
  itself, the other did not (the new consistency check now tells it). Worth a few more runs, and
  on other models.
- The AI challenge still finds real design gaps in this exercise: no contingency or nudge injects,
  a quiet period for the IT cell (H+1:34 to H+2:04), mostly email channels, no HR / works-council
  stimulus, no closure or recovery phase. These are edits for the designer, not app bugs.
- A few English strings remain in rarely used places (the unused "Global coherence" dialog, the
  internal kind shown during an Update).
- Removing the ignored `frame-ancestors` directive from the CSP `<meta>` (it only produces a console
  warning) was refused by the session's security policy; left as is.
- The test browser cannot decode H.264, so the TV inject is exported as a still image here; a
  normal Chrome exports the video.
